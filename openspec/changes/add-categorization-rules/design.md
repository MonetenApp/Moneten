# Design

## Context

This change is part of the first build of the app. The build is a Swift package with the modules `Domain`, `Storage`, `Import`, and `App`, as [ADR 0010](../../../docs/decisions/0010-swift-package-and-macos-15.md) describes. Everything runs on the Mac, with no server ([ADR 0002](../../../docs/decisions/0002-local-first-no-server.md)).

The app keeps all accounts, categories, and transactions of the open database in one in-memory `Ledger` (module `Domain`). Every change goes to the database through `Store` (module `Storage`) and then to the ledger. Views read the ledger. This keeps filters, sums, and charts fast for some ten thousand transactions without queries per keystroke.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** a rule language that accepts every example of the spec, exact error positions, and one place that decides the category of a transaction.

**Non-Goals:** none. A refresh runs the rules through `Store.merge`, which hands the `Categorizer` to `reconcile`, as a file import does.

## Decisions

- `Sources/Domain/Rule.swift` holds a tokenizer and a recursive descent parser. Precedence is `NOT`, then `AND`, then `OR`. Two expressions without an operator are joined with `AND`.
- The tokenizer reads `and`, `or`, `not`, `true`, and `false` without quotation marks and in any letter case as operators. A quoted word is always a term. `+` between two expressions is `AND`. `!` before an expression is `NOT`, and so is `-`. A `+` or `-` before a digit belongs to the number, so `-12,50` and `+4917012345` stay one term, and `amount=-89,00` stays an amount comparison. A hyphen inside a word, as in "EC-Karte", stays part of the word, and so does a `+` or `!` directly after a letter or a digit, as in "Disney+" and "Yahoo!". A minus at the end of a word before a quotation mark or a parenthesis is `NOT`, so `Amazon-"Erstattung"` is `Amazon NOT "Erstattung"`. A field name followed by `!=` is a field operator, so `name!=` and `amount!=` keep their meaning.
- A field test takes `:`, `=`, `==`, or `:=` for a match and `!=` or `<>` for no match. An amount comparison takes `:=` as equal and `<>` as not equal. The tokenizer compares field names and `amount` without regard to case, so `Name:` is `name:`. The word right after a field operator is always the term, so `name:and` searches for "and" and `name:-x` for "-x". A lone `-`, `!`, or `+` right after a field operator and before a quotation mark, a slash, or a parenthesis, as in `name:-"Stadtwerke"` and `name:-/Stadtwerke/`, is an error whose message points to `name!=`.
- A term matches whole words. The text of a field is folded (lower case, composed Unicode, ä to ae, ß to ss) and split at white space. A phrase matches a run of words. `*` and `?` match inside one word. This is why `"Kundennummer 67890*"` is needed when a slash follows the number, as the MoneyMoney help says.
- A term without a field operator searches the name, the purpose, the booking text, the references, the creditor ID, and the comment. The account fields are searched only through `local_account:` and `remote_account:`.
- Account fields compare without spaces, so `local_account:"DE02 1203 ..."` matches. Every space separator of Unicode counts, also U+00A0 and U+202F, which some exports write between the groups of an IBAN. `TextFolding.accountKey` does this for the rules, the search, and import matching alike.
- Regular expressions are case-insensitive and run on the unfolded text of each field.
- A regular expression in a rule runs under the same `RegexWatch` as one in the search (#172). The watch counts the processor time of the calling thread, so a busy Mac does not stop a normal rule. It stops a call after about 100 ms. A parsed rule also adds the time of all its calls, and stops at `Rule.runLimit`, 1 second, because a pattern that takes 30 ms on each of 200 texts never reaches the limit of one call and would take 6 seconds. A call of 1 ms or more is a pattern that backtracks without end, because a regular expression on the text of a bank takes a few microseconds, so such calls stop the rule once they add up to 50 ms (`Rule.slowCall`, `Rule.slowLimit`). A pattern that is slow on some texts then costs about 50 ms, also when it is fast on every other text. Inside a virtual machine, such as a CI runner, the guest charges a pause of its host to the thread that runs, so a call of a few microseconds can take 60 ms of thread processor time (#398). On a Mac without a virtual machine no pause counted in our tests: with 36 busy processes and the test process on the background policy, the slowest call of the thirty rules `/Haendler0x/` to `/Haendler29x/` over 50,000 rows took 69 µs. So `Rule.counted` runs a call of 1 ms or more, or one that reached the limit of one call, once more on the same text, with a watch that looks at the clock at each progress report and stops at 1 ms. A pattern that backtracks is slow again, and both calls count. A pause rarely hits the same short call twice, so a fast second call counts alone. The check costs a slow pattern about 1 ms for each slow call, and a pattern that does not end about 1 ms once. A call that reaches the limit of the run is not checked, because that limit counts all calls. All rules of one `Categorizer` together stop when their calls of 100 µs or more (`Rule.heavyCall`) take `Categorizer.batchLimit`, 3 seconds, which bounds a batch with many slow rules. A fast call does not count, because `/Haendler1/` takes about 150 ms over 50,000 rows, so 20 normal rules over a long migration would otherwise reach the limit with no slow rule among them. The categorizer keeps a running total of these calls, so a regex call does not read the time of every rule. ICU never finishes some patterns, such as `/^+?49/`, on any text that is not empty, so the stop is kept: once one regular expression of a rule stopped, `Rule.matches` returns false for every later transaction, and `Rule.stoppedRegexPosition` names its position. The stop and the time belong to the parsed rule, so they last one run: `Categorizer` parses the rules again for each merge of one account, each entry, and each migration, and the category settings parse the rule for each count. The stop is not a match, so NOT before a stopped regular expression does not match every row.
- A rule has at most 20,000 characters (`Rule.maxLength`) and nests at most 100 parentheses and NOT operators (`Rule.maxNesting`) (#568). The parser recurses once for each level, and each walker over a parsed rule, such as `hasRegex`, `Rule.matches`, the search, and the release of the tree, recurses once for each level of the tree. "Import All Data..." stores a rule as it is, so without a limit a rule of a million `!` would overflow the stack of the thread that reads it, also in the sidebar at each unlock. All terms of one `AND` or `OR` are one node of the tree, tested in their order, so 5,000 terms side by side are 2 levels deep and not 5,000. A tree is then at most about 200 levels deep, an `OR` and an `AND` for each parenthesis, which fits into the 512 KB stack of a detached task also in a debug build. `parseTerm` reads every token but an opening parenthesis apart from `parsePrimary`, because its many messages gave `parsePrimary` a frame of 3.6 KB in a debug build, and 100 levels of parentheses then overflowed that stack. The limits lie far above a real rule: a rule that "Create Rule" extended 400 times has about 15,000 characters and no nesting. The search reads a text beyond a limit as other text that the rule language rejects, by its plain words, and joins them in one node too.
- A regular expression of a rule finds whether it matches the empty text at the first empty field of a run, under the watch of the run, and keeps the answer for the parsed rule. The parser runs no regular expression, so reading a rule with 1,000 regular expressions costs no 1,000 watched calls, and a pattern that does not end on the empty text stops the rule as it would on any other text. `Category.ruleError` keeps the result of the last parse of each category together with its rule text, so a sidebar row parses a rule again only after the rule changed. It keeps no parsed rule, because the stop and the time of a parsed rule last one run.
- A stop must not depend on the order of the rows. `Categorizer.allOrNothing` runs a batch again when a rule stopped during it, and the stopped rule then matches none of its rows. `Store.merge`, the migration, an offsetting entry, and a saved entry run their categorization through it. `reconcile` is pure, so a second run gives the same result as a first run that knew of the stop. The categorizer numbers the texts of each row once and keeps one decision for each number: the first rule that matched, whether a stopped rule stood before it, and how many stops it has seen. A later row with the same texts, and every row of a repeat, reads the decision instead of building the `RuleSubject` and testing the rules again. A stop since then changes the decision without a new test: a stopped rule before the match only marks the stop, and only a row that the stopped rule itself matched goes on with the rules after it. So no regular expression runs twice on one text, and the memo holds one entry for each row and not one for each pair of rule and row. `allOrNothing` runs the batch again only when a stop changed a decision that the batch already used, so twelve rules that stop in turn above `/Haendler1/` take one pass over 50,000 rows. A `RuleSubject` folds the words of a field and makes the `Decimal` amount only when a rule first reads them, because both took most of the time of a row, and a rule with only regular expressions reads neither. `Categorizer.categorize` keeps the category of a row that a rule sorted when a stopped rule stands before every match, so a booking never moves a row because of a stop. `RuleApplier.apply` and `RuleApplier.rebase` return no changes for a stopped rule, because "Apply to all transactions." would otherwise empty the category.
- The category settings count the matches in a detached task, 250 ms after the last change of the rule, the restriction, or the ledger, so typing and a slow rule never block the main actor. A new key cancels the task and its detached count, and `RuleApplier.matchCount` checks for the cancellation every 1,000 rows.
- `Categorizer` walks the categories in sidebar order and returns the first match. `RuleApplier` applies one changed rule to existing transactions in the two modes of the spec. It reports its progress after every 1,000 transactions, and the category settings show it. One run goes at a time. While it runs, the category settings cannot be closed or changed, and the menu commands that open a sheet are disabled. The run reads a copy of the ledger. At the end, a row that is the same as in the copy takes the result of the run, a row that a refresh changed or added during the run is checked again against the current ledger, and a row that a refresh removed drops out. A lock cancels the run, and it saves nothing.
- "Create Rule" builds its condition in `Rule.condition(for:)` and joins it to the rule of the category with `Rule.adding(_:to:)`, both in `Domain`. The name is the condition because it is what the user sees in the list. The IBAN is the fallback, because `remote_account:` compares without spaces and a card payment often has no IBAN. The rule language has no escape for a quotation mark inside a term, and a quotation mark ends the term, so each quotation mark of the name becomes `?`, which matches it. `*` and `?` in a name stay wildcards and match themselves too. A name made only of wildcards, after the quotation marks became `?`, would match nearly every transaction, so it counts as no name, and the IBAN or no condition takes its place. The app puts the new rule into the category settings, which validate it, count its matches, save it, and ask how to apply it as for a typed rule. `OR` binds weakest, so `old OR new` keeps every transaction that the old rule found without parentheses. Parentheses around the old rule would nest one level deeper with each created rule. A saved rule that does not read, such as `name:`, does not read after the join either, so the settings show the error and OK stays disabled. Parentheses would even make some such rules readable, such as `x) OR (y`, which then means something that the user never wrote.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- A regular expression that is slow only on some texts, such as `/ok|(a+)+$/`, stops the whole rule for that run, also for the rows that it would match at once. One that stays just under 100 ms on every text stops when the run reaches 1 second. A run of a heavy but normal rule over a very long history could reach 1 second too: a rule with `/Haendler1/` spends about 150 ms over 50,000 rows. The category settings and "Apply to all transactions." show the stop, so the user can fix the rule. The refresh still merges on the main actor, so a stopping rule can hold it for up to about 1 second for each regular expression rule.

- A hyphen or a slash does not split a word, so `"REWE"` does not match "REWE-Markt". A user who writes a new rule may need a `*` more often than expected. The help shows this case.
- The words and, or, not, true, and false are operators in every letter case. A rule stored by the first build, or migrated, that used one of them in lower case as a search word changes its meaning. Quotation marks around the word keep it a search word. Tasks 2.8 to 2.11 of [add-moneymoney-migration](../add-moneymoney-migration/tasks.md) add a note to the migration report for each migrated rule with and, or, or not in lower case, and for each migrated rule with true or false without quotation marks. Until they are built, such a migrated rule changes its meaning without a note.
- A term without a field no longer searches account numbers and IBANs. A rule stored by the first build, or migrated, that found transactions by an IBAN or an account number as a plain term stops matching them. `remote_account:` or `local_account:` before the number keeps it working. Tasks 2.6 and 2.7 of [add-moneymoney-migration](../add-moneymoney-migration/tasks.md) add a note to the migration report for each migrated rule with an IBAN or an account number as a term without a field. Until they are built, such a migrated rule changes its meaning without a note.
- Operators, fields, and amount comparisons in the search field have the meaning of this spec. A plain word in the search follows the search requirements of `add-transaction-search-and-filters`, so it also finds account numbers, IBANs, and the category name.
